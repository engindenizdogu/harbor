## Overview and Problem

The [BirdCLEF 2026](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/birdclef-2026) competition challenges participants to identify overlapping bird calls in noisy and continuous field recordings from the Pantanal biome. This is a hard bioacoustics problem because recordings are long, multi-species calls overlap, and real-world field noise is substantial. Although we joined late and treated this as a learning exercise rather than a competitive submission, we aimed to understand and document the winning approaches from prior editions.

## What I Built

- **Architected** a multi-task student model (EfficientNetB0) that simultaneously learns from both soft labels generated by Google's Perch v2 teacher model and hard labels from human-annotated soundscapes.
- **Implemented** knowledge distillation to transfer Perch v2's rich bioacoustic representations into a compact, trainable model — heavily inspired by [Nikita Babych's winning approach](https://www.kaggle.com/nikitababych).
- **Documented** the full pipeline end-to-end with explanations and comments, from raw audio loading and mel-spectrogram extraction to model training and inference, creating a readable reference notebook for the community.

## Key Results and Impact

- Achieved a private leaderboard score of **~0.60** after only 3 training epochs under severe compute constraints (Google Colab timeouts).
- Demonstrated that multi-task distillation (soft labels from Perch + hard labels from human annotations) acts as a powerful regularizer, preventing overfitting to the teacher's biases — as noted by community feedback.
- Published a well-commented starter notebook that bridges the gap between beginner and competitive-level solutions in bioacoustic ML.

## Core Learnings

- **Knowledge distillation with dual supervision** — combining soft (teacher) and hard (human) labels is a principled way to regularize and improve generalization on noisy audio tasks.
- **Trade-offs between model capacity and compute budget** — EfficientNetB0 strikes the right balance for limited GPU/TPU environments; larger models would require more epochs to converge.
- **Bioacoustic-specific preprocessing matters** — mel-spectrogram parameterization (hop length, n_mels, frequency range) heavily influences what spectral patterns the model can learn from bird calls.
- **Perch v2 as a foundation model for audio** — Google's Perch model provides strong general-purpose audio embeddings that transfer well to ecological monitoring tasks.
